# Research Seed: Fear, Trust and Investment Decisions

Version 0.2 · Revised 2026-11-02 · Status: deferred pending framework evaluation; not ready for execution.

Current purpose: a workshop model society for perspective-taking and reflection. Existing frameworks must first pass the research assessment protocol. No platform is selected. Execution of trades is not required; attitudes, uncertainty and feasible intentions are the observations of interest.

## Research question

How do people with different economic fears, investment experience, convictions and financial constraints interpret a shared information environment? How do disagreement, persuasion, trust and practical constraints shape their investment decisions?

Explore what happens. Do not assume an asset wins, anxiety leads to purchases, people converge or opinions must change.

## Starting perspectives

Represent the following perspectives and credible opposing positions. They are hypothetical perspectives for qualitative exploration, not a measured population distribution:

- People afraid of downward economic mobility, including those seeking tangible protection and those prioritizing cash, debt repayment or immediate expenses.
- Crash forecasters and people skeptical of repeated collapse predictions, including people willing to revise either view.
- Bitcoin enthusiasts who regard gold as obsolete, people skeptical of Bitcoin, and people open to both or neither.
- Gold advocates and people who prefer interest income, productive assets, diversification or liquid savings.
- Silver advocates and people skeptical of scarcity narratives, industrial-demand investment theses or concentrated exposure.
- People without investment experience who find physical metal understandable, and equally inexperienced people who prefer familiar banking or avoid investing altogether.
- People who value jewelry both for use and as emergency savings, and people who question its resale value or prefer standardized reserve assets.
- People influenced by popular narratives, contrarian voices and people with low interest in financial discussion.

Do not equate anxiety, inexperience or jewelry ownership with irrationality. Skeptics and experienced investors can also be biased or mistaken. Do not infer real cultural or demographic prevalence from these perspectives.

The selected framework should develop distinct individual personas from these perspectives, including overlapping identities and credible financial constraints. Do not make each group one unanimous speaker. Do not prescribe individual trades, future reactions, argument winners or changes of mind. Initial preferences are revisable.

## Shared information environment

Use the existing research as background information: metal supply and demand, recycling and substitution, real interest rates, reserve allocation, energy and infrastructure, historical market data and attributed investment interpretations. The information environment may contain conflicting interpretations; source facts retain their provenance, dates and uncertainty.

See the repository's existing evidence pack and fact register. They are an archived research vintage, not automatically current observations. A compact selection and its exact source references must be fixed before import. Contemporary geopolitical claims require separately verified sources. Fictional event updates must be labeled as hypothetical.

Institutions and market-sector entities supply context. The conversational focus is the people interpreting that context. Do not automatically turn every named publisher, commodity or institution into an additional speaking agent.

Information can be available without being read, trusted or acted upon. Preserve this distinction in the record. Let actors seek information and engage with other perspectives; do not predetermine who persuades whom or which voices gain attention.

## What the selected framework should develop

- Individual interpretations, questions, arguments and misunderstandings.
- Interactions, disagreements, trust changes and emerging attention patterns.
- Stable convictions, revisions, unexpected alliances and unresolved differences.
- Feasible decisions, including seeking information, holding, buying, selling, retaining cash, reducing debt and choosing not to act.

These are simulated possibilities. They are not survey results, actual expert testimony or evidence of real sentiment prevalence.

## Observation and evaluation

Separate emotional response, expected asset performance, stated preference, intended action and feasible action. A person may fear a crash yet sell an asset to meet expenses. Whether this actually emerges must remain open.

Persist actions and memory, with actor identity, round, received information, interaction references and decision rationale where available. Subsequent interviews should distinguish remembered behavior from new reflection. Missing evidence stays missing; the reporter must not fill gaps with plausible stories.

The output should explain what happened between actors and cite examples. Identify what remained unchanged, what contradicted expectations and what was merely restated from the input. Do not substitute a generic macroeconomic essay for these observations.

No calendar-year forecast, generated market-clearing prices, real population shares or calibrated probabilities are required. Financial amounts may not be invented without a documented budget scheme.

## Lessons from the first attempt

The earlier report largely repeated design-level relationships without agent evidence. It did not demonstrate comparative scenario outcomes. The first attempt is treated as an unsuccessful method trial for the intended knowledge goal, not as a successful market forecast. No investment conclusion from it receives additional evidential weight here.

## Before a pilot

Select the compact research input, verify imported persona diversity, and confirm that action records and memories remain accessible after completion. Keep the initial pilot bounded. Use a direct analysis of the same material as a comparison: any additional computational cost must earn additional, traceable insight.
